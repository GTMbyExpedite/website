# Copy Rules for Website Editing

These rules apply when writing or rewriting any text on a website: headlines, descriptions, bullet points, CTAs, section ledes, bios, and labels.

## Outcomes over features

The reader wants to know what they get, not what the thing abstractly "is."

**Weak (feature/category):**
- "Outbound infrastructure and segmentation"
- "Senior GTM judgement on your key decisions"
- "Comprehensive analytics dashboard"

**Strong (outcome/deliverable):**
- "Sending domains, sequences and audience segments built and ready to run"
- "Your go-to-market plan reviewed and stress-tested"
- "A dashboard showing which accounts are moving and which stalled"

The test: can the reader picture the deliverable? If it sounds like a category label on a features page, rewrite it.

## Sentence structure

- **One idea per sentence.** Don't pack two insights into a balanced construction. "This is not X. It's Y." is a crutch — just state Y.
- **Active voice by default.** "We build your pipeline" not "Your pipeline will be built." Passive voice is fine when the actor genuinely doesn't matter.
- **Front-load the point.** Lead with what matters. Cut throat-clearing openers ("Here's the thing," "It's worth noting," "At the end of the day").
- **Plain connectors.** Use periods and commas. Semicolons are fine between genuinely parallel clauses. Colons for lists and labels, not for dramatic reveals.

## Density and rhythm

- **No em dashes in short copy.** Bullets, CTAs, single-sentence descriptions: use a period or comma instead. In paragraphs of 3+ sentences, one em dash is acceptable.
- **Watch slogan density.** Two punchy one-liners near each other is fine. Three or more creates a wall of compressed wisdom that reads as performative. Break the run with a plain explanatory sentence.
- **Alternate compression with expansion.** After a tight punchy line, follow with a sentence that unpacks it or adds a concrete detail. This rhythm (punch, explain, punch, explain) reads as confident without being exhausting.

## Specificity

- **Numbers over adjectives.** "60 to 90 days" not "quickly." "First 100 accounts" not "a focused set."
- **Named deliverables over abstractions.** "A named account list" not "strategic targeting." "Weekly calls with Kartik" not "ongoing strategic alignment."
- **Real verbs over weak verb phrases.** "Decided" not "made a decision." "Can" not "has the ability to."

## Uniformity in lists

- All items in a bullet list should be the same kind of thing: all outcomes, all deliverables, or all process descriptions. Don't mix "Your team trained on the playbook" (outcome) with "Weekly strategy calls" (process) in the same list.
- If items naturally group into different types, use separate labelled lists ("What you get" vs "How we work").
- Keep items to roughly the same length. One item at 5 words and another at 30 looks unbalanced.
- Max 4 items per list in marketing context. More than that means the items are too granular or the section is overloaded.
